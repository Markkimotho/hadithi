# Hadithi: the anthology

All episodes use newly written English prose and original vector illustrations. They adapt narrative motifs, not a digitized book's text or images. The new locations, puzzles, choices, and alternative endings are game fiction. Each story has a turn-based world; time advances only through an action with a stated cost. Inspection and reading clues are free. A single optional genre shift changes events, available solutions, and ending conditions. Since the second edition every tale also has seeded dice checks with visible odds, clue deductions, cross-run echoes, a secret ending reachable only with remembered knowledge, characters, marks, a map, and authored weather and light.

## The Wisdom in the Pot

Source: “How Wisdom Became the Property of the Human Race,” in _West African Folk-Tales_, W. H. Barker and Cecilia Sinclair, 1917. [Text](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/66923/pg66923-images.html). The source names Fanti-land, in present-day Ghana, and Kweku Tsin as Anansi's son. We use Fante as the modern context label. The collection's colonial introduction is not reproduced or endorsed. Its unnamed oral storytellers deserve acknowledgement; the compilers are not the originators of the tradition.

Play as Kweku Tsin, following Anansi's attempt to hide a pot of wisdom. Collect cord, cloth, a gourd, and a reed; repair a carrying sling; follow tracks; cross a changing river; decide whether to expose, protect, or bargain over the pot. A descending rainstorm changes the river and a favor brings a ferryman later. The knot puzzle teaches how to carry rather than magically conquer the pot. Endings: shared wisdom, a negotiated keeper, or scattered fragments. The source's thrown-pot outcome is one possibility, rather than a mandatory judgment. Second edition: dice at the stepping stones, the climb, the storm, and the council (persuading the circle to hear Anansi); four deductions (why he carries the pot in front, why he wanted to be followed, how tired he is, what the second seal means). Secret ending *The pot that asked for a story* requires the echo of the scattered ending, the child's story.

## The Palace Beneath the Tide

Source: “The Story of Urashima Taro, the Fisher Lad,” _Japanese Fairy Tales_, Yei Theodora Ozaki, first published 1903; consulted electronic edition includes a 1908 preface. [Text](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/4018/pg4018-images.html). Ozaki explains her retellings draw on Sadanami Sanjin and are adapted for Western readers. The game retains the rescued turtle, underwater palace, time displacement, and sealed box; its tide mechanism and negotiated return are new fiction. It does not claim to represent all Japanese beliefs or tellings.

Play as Urashima. Free a turtle from fishing line, decide how to travel, investigate a palace whose four seasons hide a clock, and choose what home means. Objects: line, knife, shell, time chart, sealed box. A turn-based tide interrupts exploration; trust changes whether the turtle can guide a return. The seasonal dial puzzle has an explicit clue and retry path. Endings: a return with an honest cost, a new life beneath the sea, or a return to an unfamiliar century. Second edition: the feast is a clock (each course costs a season unless you understand it and refuse); dice on the bright current, the surge, pressing Otohime, and steering home by a remembered morning. Secret ending *A door in the tide* returns the unopened box, using the echo of the century ending.

## The Midnight Orchestra

Source: “The Travelling Musicians,” _Grimms' Fairy Tales_, Jacob and Wilhelm Grimm, consulted English edition based on Edgar Taylor and Marian Edwardes. [Text](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/2591/pg2591-images.html). The German tale is commonly called the Bremen Town Musicians. Playable negotiation, the stolen ledger, patrols, and community concert are new fiction. Animal characters have their own goals and competence; age is not a failure condition.

Play as the donkey, recruit dog, cat, and rooster, investigate a house occupied by thieves, and conduct a diversion or gather evidence. Objects: bread, bell, cord, whistle, ledger. A rehearsal puzzle orders the four voices using a visible clue. The patrol returns after actions; a shared meal changes whether an ally will cover the door. Endings: a home for the ensemble, a case carried to town, or escape with a broken plan. Second edition: dice for rushing the gate, the floorboards, improvising, and bluffing the returning thief, with patrol alert counting against each; deductions link the ledger to the sacks, the broken wheel to the miller, and one lantern to an unwatched back door. Secret ending *Bremen, at last* finishes the journey the source never completes; it needs echoes from two other endings.

## The Olive Jar

Source: “Story of Ali Cogia, Merchant of Bagdad,” *The Arabian Nights Entertainments*, edited by Andrew Lang, 1898, from Antoine Galland's French. [Catalog](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/128) · [text](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/128/pg128-images.html). Set in Baghdad under Harun al-Rashid. Ali Cogia is one of the tales Galland added to his *Mille et une nuits* that are not found in the older Arabic manuscripts. Many of those additions are attributed to the Aleppine storyteller Hanna Diyab; the attribution of this particular tale is not established, and the game does not claim one. The tale is labelled by city and collection, not as a stand-in for a national or regional tradition. The source ends with the thief sentenced to death; this adaptation ends in restitution and does not depict execution. Religious practice (the pilgrimage, the call to prayer) is mentioned respectfully as setting and is never a puzzle, item, or joke.

Play as a quick child of the courtyard who stages the children's game of cadi. Investigate the inn, the merchant's shop, his wife's door, the olive row, the cadi's court, and the fountain before moonrise forces the game to begin. Objects: an olive, the jar, a palm-leaf tag, a bribe. Six deductions, including the tale's key one (sealed seven years ago, this year's olives). A bribe interrupt fires when the merchant's suspicion rises. A casting puzzle assigns four children their parts. Themes: Wonder, Mystery, and Reconciliation. Endings: a just verdict, a reconciliation through the merchant's wife, an oath that outruns the truth, and the secret *Strangers in the corner* (needs echoes from a won case and a lost one).
