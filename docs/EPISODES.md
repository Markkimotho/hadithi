# Hadithi: first anthology

All episodes use newly written English prose and original vector illustrations. They adapt narrative motifs, not a digitized book's text or images. The new locations, puzzles, choices, and alternative endings are game fiction. Each story has a turn-based world; time advances only through an action with a stated cost. Inspection and reading clues are free. A single optional genre shift changes events, available solutions, and ending conditions.

## The Wisdom in the Pot

Source: “How Wisdom Became the Property of the Human Race,” in _West African Folk-Tales_, W. H. Barker and Cecilia Sinclair, 1917. [Text](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/66923/pg66923-images.html). The source names Fanti-land, in present-day Ghana, and Kweku Tsin as Anansi's son. We use Fante as the modern context label. The collection's colonial introduction is not reproduced or endorsed. Its unnamed oral storytellers deserve acknowledgement; the compilers are not the originators of the tradition.

Play as Kweku Tsin, following Anansi's attempt to hide a pot of wisdom. Collect cord, cloth, a gourd, and a reed; repair a carrying sling; follow tracks; cross a changing river; decide whether to expose, protect, or bargain over the pot. A descending rainstorm changes the river and a favor brings a ferryman later. The knot puzzle teaches how to carry rather than magically conquer the pot. Endings: shared wisdom, a negotiated keeper, or scattered fragments. The source's thrown-pot outcome is one possibility, rather than a mandatory judgment.

## The Palace Beneath the Tide

Source: “The Story of Urashima Taro, the Fisher Lad,” _Japanese Fairy Tales_, Yei Theodora Ozaki, first published 1903; consulted electronic edition includes a 1908 preface. [Text](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/4018/pg4018-images.html). Ozaki explains her retellings draw on Sadanami Sanjin and are adapted for Western readers. The game retains the rescued turtle, underwater palace, time displacement, and sealed box; its tide mechanism and negotiated return are new fiction. It does not claim to represent all Japanese beliefs or tellings.

Play as Urashima. Free a turtle from fishing line, decide how to travel, investigate a palace whose four seasons hide a clock, and choose what home means. Objects: line, knife, shell, time chart, sealed box. A turn-based tide interrupts exploration; trust changes whether the turtle can guide a return. The seasonal dial puzzle has an explicit clue and retry path. Endings: a return with an honest cost, a new life beneath the sea, or a return to an unfamiliar century.

## The Midnight Orchestra

Source: “The Travelling Musicians,” _Grimms' Fairy Tales_, Jacob and Wilhelm Grimm, consulted English edition based on Edgar Taylor and Marian Edwardes. [Text](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/2591/pg2591-images.html). The German tale is commonly called the Bremen Town Musicians. Playable negotiation, the stolen ledger, patrols, and community concert are new fiction. Animal characters have their own goals and competence; age is not a failure condition.

Play as the donkey, recruit dog, cat, and rooster, investigate a house occupied by thieves, and conduct a diversion or gather evidence. Objects: bread, bell, cord, whistle, ledger. A rehearsal puzzle orders the four voices using a visible clue. The patrol returns after actions; a shared meal changes whether an ally will cover the door. Endings: a home for the ensemble, a case carried to town, or escape with a broken plan.
