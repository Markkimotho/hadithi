# How a telling works

## The storyteller

A storyteller tells each tale as you play: how it opens, the name of each new place, what you find, what people say and what it costs you. Their words appear in a small panel (bottom left on a computer, at the top on a phone) so the world stays in view. When a person speaks, their face is beside their name and their words float over their head.

Read at your own pace and press **E** to go on. Press **H** to fold the panel to a single line while you explore. Conversations and trials always open in full, because they need your answer.

## Walking costs nothing

Walking, looking around, lifting and throwing things, and reading your journal never spend time. **Doing** something in the story does: asking, taking, persuading, travelling. Each takes a turn.

The world keeps its own schedule. Storms come, the tide turns, guards walk their rounds and the moon rises after so many turns, whether you are ready or not.

## Acting

A gold diamond hangs over everything you can look at, take or talk to. A blue diamond marks something you remember from an earlier telling. A grey one is not open to you yet; walk up to it and the prompt says what you still need.

Put the crosshair on it and press **E**. Your hero acts it out (kneels to look, reaches to take, turns to talk) and the storyteller tells what happens. Things you take fly into your bag, shown in the corner of the screen. A person with several things to say opens a conversation: choose a line with the number keys, or leave it for now.

## Loose things

Pots, jars, baskets and stools can be pushed around, lifted (**Q**) and thrown (**F**). Throw a pot hard enough and it cracks, and the story notices.

## Trials

Leaps across a river, climbs, holding your ground in a storm, sneaking past sleepers and winning an argument are played by hand. A ring closes on each moment: press **E** as it meets its mark. To win an argument, lay what you have learned on the elders' scale.

- What you have found and chosen makes a trial easier, and the odds are shown before you start.
- A **one try** trial can't be repeated. A **retry** trial opens again once your odds improve.
- Failing always moves the story on, just differently. There is no game over.
- Backing out of a trial before it ends changes nothing.

Prefer dice? Turn **Physical trials** off in Settings and seeded dice decide instead, at the same odds.

## The journal

Press **J**. It has four pages:

- **Bag**: what you carry. Some things combine into something new.
- **What you know**: everything you have noticed. Choose two things that fit together and **Connect** them. A true connection is a discovery that opens new conversations, arguments and endings. A wrong one costs nothing.
- **People**: who you have met and what they want.
- **Map**: the places of the tale and the roads between them.

## The turn of the tale

Once in each telling, from the bottom of the journal, you may turn the tale toward a different kind of story: wonder, mystery, survival or reconciliation, depending on the tale. It changes what can happen from there: routes, solutions and endings, not just the mood. It takes a turn.

## Endings, echoes and marks

Each tale has four endings, one of them secret. An ending closes the telling with a closing page.

- **Echoes.** Every ending teaches an echo. Begin the tale again with **Carry echoes** on and it remembers: blue **◈ Remembered** actions appear that were not there before. Each secret ending can only be reached with what an earlier telling taught you.
- **Marks.** Marks recognise how you played: travelling light, thinking clearly, keeping the peace. Each tale has five.
- **The chronicle** (on the title screen) lists every ending you have found, every echo and mark, and your recent tellings.

## Taking back a step

Press **U** to take back your last step. What luck decided stays decided: undoing a failed trial does not let you roll again for free.
